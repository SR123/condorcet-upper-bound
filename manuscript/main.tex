\begin{abstract}
We reduce full Condorcet domains to adjacent-swap components defined by
extreme never-conditions and fixed pair comparisons. Their deletion sums
add to the original deletion sum plus its insertion gaps. Combining this
identity with Moros's gap inequality and an exact finite-window estimate
gives $f(n)\le32n\,f(n-1)/(11n+8)$, hence
$f(n)=O(n^{-8/11}(32/11)^n)$. The same argument recovers the peak-pit
growth bound $8/3$. Ordered concatenation removes the window boundary
loss. The finite certificate and exact checking programs are supplied
separately.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A \emph{Condorcet domain} is a family of linear orders for which pairwise
majority is transitive for every odd voter profile. Let $f(n)$ be its
maximum possible size on $n$ alternatives. By Sen's characterization
\cite{Sen}, each triple must have an alternative that never occupies
one of its three positions. We write $\nc xp$ for such a
\emph{never-condition}. A domain is \emph{peak-pit} if every triple has
a never-first or never-last condition; let $h(n)$ be the corresponding
maximum. No common axis is required.

Raz~\cite{Raz} proved that $f(n)$ is at most exponential. Moros's
manuscript~\cite{Moros} introduced the deletion-and-gap framework used
here and obtained explicit general bases $56/19$ and $1336/455$.
It also proved the peak-pit base $8/3$, using finite windows and
potential cancellation. Our numerical improvement concerns the
unrestricted bound; the peak-pit base is recovered within the same
structural argument. For background and constructions, see
\cite{Fishburn,KarpovSlinko,KMRZ}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $n\ge3$,
\begin{align}
 f(n)&\le\frac{32n}{11n+8}f(n-1),&
 f(n)&\le4(32/11)^{n-3},\label{eq:general}\\
 h(n)&\le\frac83h(n-1),&
 h(n)&\le4(8/3)^{n-3}.\label{eq:peak}
\end{align}
The limits of $f(n)^{1/n}$ and $h(n)^{1/n}$ exist and are at most
$32/11=2.9090909\ldots$ and $8/3$, respectively. Moreover,
$f(n)=O(n^{-8/11}(32/11)^n)$.
\end{theorem}

The structural step is to reduce general domains to families defined
by extreme never-conditions and fixed pair comparisons. Write $D_{-x}$
for the distinct orders left after deleting $x$, and put
\[
 M=|D|,\qquad \del(D)=\sum_x|D_{-x}|.
\]
Every deletion preserves the Condorcet property, so a maximum domain
satisfies $\del(D)\le nf(n-1)$. For a full domain $D$, we prove that its
adjacent-swap components $K$ belong to the extreme-law class and satisfy
\[
 \sum_K\del(K)=\del(D)+G(D),
\]
where $G(D)$ counts gaps between runs of insertion positions. This
component reduction is the main structural contribution of the paper.
It builds on Moros's framework and uses the median structure
studied by Puppe and Slinko~\cite{PuppeSlinko}.

The extreme-law estimate $\del(K)\ge3n|K|/8$ therefore gives
$\del(D)+G(D)\ge3n|D|/8$. Moros's maximal-domain gap inequality
$G(D)\le3\del(D)-(n+1)|D|$ then yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
 \frac38n|D|\le\del(D)+G(D)
 \le4\del(D)-(n+1)|D|,
 \qquad \del(D)\ge\frac{11n+8}{32}|D|.
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} records the same transfer with an arbitrary
uniform density input. Sections~\ref{sec:components} and~\ref{sec:gaps}
give the structural and gap arguments. Section~\ref{sec:finite} retains
the finite lemma, its exceptional case and coverage proof;
Section~\ref{sec:finish} removes the boundary loss. Exact certificate
tables, witness files and reproduction instructions are separate
supplementary material.

\section{Fibres and adjacent-swap components}\label{sec:components}

A \emph{full domain} is the set $D(N)$ of all orders satisfying one
specified never-condition on each triple. Every inclusion-maximal
Condorcet domain is full: choose a common condition on each triple,
and use maximality. In auxiliary arguments we also allow fixed pair
comparisons. Let $\F_n$ be the nonempty full domains defined by one
extreme condition ($\nc x1$ or $\nc x3$) per triple and any fixed pairs.
Pair conditions are necessary because conditioning an outside order
can create them inside a window.

Fix $x$ and $P\in D_{-x}$. Its \emph{insertion fibre} is
\[
 F(x,P)=\{t\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}:P[x:t]\in D\},
\]
where $P[x:t]$ inserts $x$ with $t$ alternatives above it. If this set
has $r$ nonempty consecutive runs, it contributes $r-1$ to $G(D)$.
Let $A(D)$ count enabled adjacent swaps over every order and position;
thus each undirected edge is counted twice.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:fibre}
For every finite family of orders,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:runidentity}
 \del(D)+G(D)=n|D|-A(D).
\end{equation}
Every fibre of a domain in $\F_n$ is an interval, so $G(D)=0$ there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A fibre with $m$ occupied slots and $r$ runs has $m-r$ neighbouring
occupied pairs. Each corresponds to moving its distinguished alternative
down by one slot. Summed over all distinguished alternatives, these moves
count every directed swap exactly once. Hence
$A=nM-\sum_F r$, and $\sum_F r=\del+G$.
For an extreme law, the admissible insertion slots form an interval;
the same is true of a fixed comparison. Intersect these intervals.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Component reduction]\label{thm:components}
Every adjacent-swap component $K$ of a full Condorcet domain belongs to
$\F_n$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:componentidentity}
 \sum_K\del(K)=\del(D)+G(D).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A full domain is closed under coordinatewise majority of pair comparisons.
This is the median structure of closed Condorcet domains studied
by Puppe and Slinko~\cite{PuppeSlinko}; we give the component argument here.
Indeed, the Condorcet property makes that majority transitive, and every
never-condition is preserved by majority. For never-first and never-last
this follows from the intersection of two subsets of size two among three
voters; never-middle says that the distinguished alternative's two
comparisons agree.

Let $U$ be all comparisons constant on $K$. We first show that $K$
contains every order of $D$ agreeing with $U$. Otherwise choose such a
$\tau$ outside $K$ and $\sigma\in K$ closest to it in comparison distance.
A differing comparison is not constant on $K$, so some $\rho\in K$
agrees with $\tau$ there. Project a path from $\sigma$ to $\rho$ by
$v\mapsto\maj(\sigma,\tau,v)$. The images remain in $D$, and consecutive
images differ in at most one comparison. If two linear orders differ in only one pair comparison, that pair is
adjacent in both orders. Thus the image path stays in $K$,
while the final image is closer to $\tau$, a contradiction.

A selected never-middle law $\nc x2$ forces the comparisons of $x$
with the other two alternatives to agree.
Neither can change along an adjacent-swap path, so both are fixed on $K$.
Replace this law by the extreme law implied by their common value, keep
the other extreme laws, and impose $U$. The resulting full solution set
is exactly $K$. Thus $K\in\F_n$.

An original fibre meets each component in either no slots or one run:
it is an interval within a component, and consecutive slots are joined
by swaps. Its $r$ runs are consequently counted $r$ times in the
component deletion sums. Summing gives~\eqref{eq:componentidentity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Transfer of a density estimate]\label{prop:transfer}
Fix $n\ge3$ and $\alpha>0$. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha}
 \del(K)\ge\alpha n|K|\quad\hbox{for every }K\in\F_n.
\end{equation}
Then every inclusion-maximal Condorcet domain $D$ on $n$ alternatives
satisfies
\[
 \del(D)\ge\frac{(1+\alpha)n+1}{4}|D|.
\]
Consequently,
\[
 f(n)\le\frac{4n}{(1+\alpha)n+1}f(n-1),
 \qquad h(n)\le\alpha^{-1}h(n-1).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A maximal domain is full. The component identity gives
$\del(D)+G(D)\ge\alpha n|D|$; Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}, proved next,
bounds the left side by $4\del(D)-(n+1)|D|$. Apply the resulting
inequality to a domain of size $f(n)$ and use
$\del(D)\le nf(n-1)$. A maximum peak-pit domain can be completed under
one chosen extreme law per triple. The completion is still peak-pit,
so maximal size makes it equal to the original domain. It belongs to
$\F_n$, and~\eqref{eq:alpha} gives the second recurrence.
\end{proof}

This is an algebraic formulation of the present argument. Any stronger
uniform density estimate for this same extreme-law class would improve
the unrestricted recurrence through the displayed formula. It does not
require a change to the component reduction or gap inequality.

\section{Moros's gap inequality}\label{sec:gaps}

\begin{theorem}[Gap inequality]\label{thm:gap}
For every inclusion-maximal Condorcet domain $D$ on $n\ge3$ alternatives,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
 G(D)\le3\del(D)-(n+1)|D|.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

We use Moros's shorter proof~\cite[Sections 3--4]{Moros}: two exact counts
reduce the inequality to an injection from free alternatives into locked adjacent pairs.
Let $D$ be a set of orders and $\sigma=s_1\cdots s_n\in D$. A \emph{lift} of $x$ in $\sigma$ is an order of $D$
obtained by moving $x$ upwards and keeping the other alternatives in their relative order; a \emph{drop} is defined
symmetrically. The \emph{lowest lift} is the lift that places $x$ lowest, and the \emph{highest drop} the drop that
places it highest. We call $x$ \emph{blocked} in $\sigma$ if it has no lift, \emph{free} if it has a lift and a drop,
and \emph{frozen} if it has neither. Letter $j\in[n-1]$ of $\sigma$ is the adjacent pair $(s_j,s_{j+1})$. If
$\sw_j(\sigma)\in D$, it is a lift of $s_{j+1}$ and a drop of $s_j$. Letter $j$ is \emph{locked} if $s_{j+1}$ is
blocked and $s_j$ has no drop: neither can pass the other. Let $\mathrm{fr}(D)$ and $\mathrm{fz}(D)$ be the numbers of
pairs $(\sigma,x)$ with $x$ free, respectively frozen, in $\sigma\in D$, and $\mathrm{lk}(D)$ the number of locked
letters $(\sigma,j)$. Recall that $A(D)$ counts the pairs $(\sigma,j)$ with $\sw_j(\sigma)\in D$.

Within one fibre of $x$, the member placing $x$ highest is the only one in which $x$ is blocked, and the member placing
it lowest is the only one in which $x$ has no drop. Summing over all fibres (cf.~\cite{Fathi}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
 \del(D)=\#\{(\sigma,x):x\text{ is blocked in }\sigma\}=\#\{(\sigma,x):x\text{ has no drop in }\sigma\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:missing}
Let $D$ be a full domain, $\sigma\in D$ and $\sw_j(\sigma)\notin D$. Then $s_{j+1}$ is blocked in $\sigma$ or $s_j$
has no drop in $\sigma$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $u=s_j$ and $w=s_{j+1}$. If fixed pairs are present, violating their
comparison blocks both moves. Otherwise the swap changes only the comparison
of $u$ and $w$, so it violates the law of some
triple $T=\{u,w,y\}$, and $y$ is above both or below both. If $y$ is below, $\sigma$ and $\sw_j(\sigma)$ give $uwy$ and
$wuy$ on $T$, so the law is $\nc w1$ or $\nc u2$. A lift of $w$ also gives $wuy$, so $w$ has none. If $y$ is above,
they give $yuw$ and $ywu$, so the law is $\nc u3$ or $\nc w2$. A drop of $u$ also gives $ywu$, so $u$ has none.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:twocounts}
For every finite set $D$ of orders,
\[
 2\del(D)=n|D|-\mathrm{fr}(D)+\mathrm{fz}(D),
\]
and if $D$ is a full domain, also
\[
 2\del(D)=(n+1)|D|-A(D)+\mathrm{lk}(D).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:count}, $2\del(D)$ counts the pairs $(\sigma,x)$ in which $x$ is blocked plus those in which $x$ has no
drop. A frozen pair is counted twice, a free pair never, and every other pair once; this is the first identity. For the
second, give each blocked $s_{j+1}$ and each $s_j$ without a drop to letter $j$ of $\sigma$. Only the top of each
order, which is blocked, and the bottom, which has no drop, are left over. A letter with $\sw_j(\sigma)\in D$ receives
nothing and a locked letter receives two. By Lemma~\ref{lem:missing} every other letter receives exactly one. So
$2\del(D)=2|D|+(n-1)|D|-A(D)+\mathrm{lk}(D)$.
\end{proof}

The first identity is the sum over $x$ of Fathi's fibre identity~\cite[eq.~(96)]{Fathi}; the summed form appears in
the proof of~\cite[Theorem~K.189]{Fathi}. Adding the two identities and using Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre},
$A(D)=n|D|-\del(D)-G(D)$, gives for every full domain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slackid}
 3\del(D)-(n+1)|D|-G(D)=\mathrm{fz}(D)+\mathrm{lk}(D)-\mathrm{fr}(D).
\end{equation}
So Theorem~\ref{thm:gap} follows once the free pairs are sent injectively into the locked letters. The second
identity and Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre} also give $\del(D)=|D|+G(D)+\mathrm{lk}(D)$ for every full domain.

The injection uses maximality through the following observation.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:adj}
Let $D$ be a maximal Condorcet domain in which $p$ is above $q$ in every order. Then no order of $D$ has $p$ directly
above $q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\rho\in D$ have $p$ directly above $q$, and let $\rho'$ be $\rho$ with $p$ and $q$ exchanged; $\rho'\notin D$. On
every triple $\{p,q,y\}$, $D$ satisfies $\nc p3$ and $\nc q1$, and $\rho'$ gives $yqp$ or $qpy$, which satisfy
$\nc q1$, respectively $\nc p3$. Other triples are ordered by $\rho'$ as by $\rho$. So $D\cup\{\rho'\}$ is a Condorcet
domain, against maximality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:pull}
Let $D$ be a Condorcet domain, $b=s_k$ free in $\sigma\in D$, $a=s_{k-1}$, $c=s_{k+1}$ and $T=\{a,b,c\}$. Then $D$
satisfies $\nc a3$ or $\nc c1$ on $T$. If $D$ satisfies $\nc a3$ on $T$, then in every lift of $b$ the alternative $a$
is directly above $c$, and the letter $(a,c)$ is locked. If $D$ satisfies $\nc c1$ on $T$, the same holds in every
drop of $b$. If $D$ is maximal, it does not satisfy both.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $T$, $\sigma$ gives $abc$, a lift of $b$ gives $bac$ and a drop gives $acb$. So $b$ takes every position, $a$ only
the first two and $c$ only the last two; the only laws these orders allow are $\nc a3$ and $\nc c1$. In a lift $\tau$
of $b$, the alternatives from the new place of $b$ down to $a$ move down one place, so $a$ is directly above $c$. A lift
of $c$ or a drop of $a$ in $\tau$ would put $c$ above $a$, with $b$ still above $a$: $a$ last on $T$. In a drop $\tau$
of $b$, $a$ is directly above $c$ and $b$ is below $c$; a lift of $c$ or a drop of $a$ in $\tau$ would put $c$ first
on $T$. Finally, the orders of $T$ allowed by both laws are $abc$, $acb$ and $bac$, so $a$ would be above $c$ in every
order of $D$, while a lift of $b$ has $a$ directly above $c$, against Lemma~\ref{lem:adj}.
\end{proof}

The first assertion is~\cite[Lemma~K.52]{Fathi}. Now let $D$ be maximal. For a free pair $(\sigma,b)$, with $a,c,T$ as
in Lemma~\ref{lem:pull}, let $\tau$ be the lowest lift of $b$ if $D$ satisfies $\nc a3$ on $T$, and the highest drop of
$b$ otherwise. Put $\psi(\sigma,b)=(\tau,j)$, where $j$ is the position of $a$ in $\tau$: the letter $(a,c)$ of $\tau$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:free}
If $D$ is a maximal Condorcet domain, $\psi$ maps the free pairs of $D$ injectively to its locked letters. So
$\mathrm{fr}(D)\le\mathrm{lk}(D)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The images are locked letters by Lemma~\ref{lem:pull}. Let $(\tau,j)$ be a locked letter, $p=s_j(\tau)$,
$q=s_{j+1}(\tau)$, and $(\sigma,b)$ a preimage. Then $b\notin\{p,q\}$, and $\sigma$ is $\tau$ with $b$ moved into the
slot between $p$ and $q$; so $b$ determines the preimage. If $\psi(\sigma,b)$ used a lift, then $b$ is above $p$ in
$\tau$, $D$ satisfies $\nc p3$ and not $\nc q1$ on $\{p,q,b\}$, and $b$ has a drop in $\sigma$; call $b$
\emph{upper}. Otherwise $b$ is below $q$ in $\tau$, $D$ satisfies $\nc q1$ and not $\nc p3$ on $\{p,q,b\}$, and $b$ has
a lift in $\sigma$; call $b$ \emph{lower}. For upper $b$, some $\chi_b\in D$ puts $q$ first on $\{p,q,b\}$, and as $p$
is never last there, $\chi_b$ gives $qpb$. Suppose that $b_1\ne b_2$ give preimages $(\sigma_1,b_1)$ and
$(\sigma_2,b_2)$.

\emph{Both upper.} Let $\rho_k$ be a drop of $b_k$ in $\sigma_k$. On $\{b_1,b_2,q\}$, $\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\rho_1,\rho_2$
give $b_2b_1q$, $b_1b_2q$, $b_2qb_1$, $b_1qb_2$. So $b_1$ and $b_2$ take every position, and $D$ satisfies $\nc q1$
there. In $\chi_{b_1}$, $q$ is above $b_1$, hence below $b_2$, so $\chi_{b_1}$ gives $b_2qp$ on $\{p,q,b_2\}$, against
$\nc p3$.

\emph{Both lower.} Reversing every order keeps $D$ a maximal Condorcet domain, exchanges lifts and drops (the lowest
lift becomes the highest drop) and maps locked letters to locked letters. As $D$ never satisfies both laws of
Lemma~\ref{lem:pull}, the rule defining $\psi$ is symmetric, so reversal maps $\psi$ to $\psi$ and exchanges upper and
lower. Apply the previous case.

\emph{$b_1$ upper, $b_2$ lower.} On $\{p,q,b_1,b_2\}$, $\tau$ gives $b_1pqb_2$, $\sigma_1$ gives $pb_1qb_2$, $\sigma_2$
gives $b_1pb_2q$, and $\chi=\chi_{b_1}$, in which $q$ is above $p$, has $b_2$ above $q$ (by $\nc q1$ on
$\{p,q,b_2\}$): it gives $b_2qpb_1$. Let $\rho$ be a drop of $b_1$ in $\sigma_1$ and $\lambda$ a lift of $b_2$ in
$\sigma_2$. On $\{b_1,b_2,q\}$, $\tau,\sigma_2,\chi$ give $b_1qb_2$, $b_1b_2q$, $b_2qb_1$, and $\rho$ puts $q$ first.
So $b_2$ and $q$ take every position, $D$ satisfies $\nc{b_1}2$ there, $\rho$ gives $qb_2b_1$ and $\lambda$ gives
$b_1b_2q$. On $\{p,b_1,b_2\}$, $\tau,\sigma_1,\rho,\lambda,\chi$ then give $b_1pb_2$, $pb_1b_2$, $pb_2b_1$, $b_1b_2p$,
$b_2pb_1$: each of $p,b_1,b_2$ takes every position, which is impossible in a Condorcet domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gap}]
A maximal domain is full, so~\eqref{eq:slackid} holds, and its right-hand side is nonnegative by
Proposition~\ref{prop:free}.
\end{proof}

\section{A finite window inequality}\label{sec:finite}

For $H\in\F_k$, its \emph{bottom slices} group orders by their last
alternative and delete it; top slices group by the first alternative.
Every nonempty slice belongs to $\F_{k-1}$. Write $a_j(H)$ for the
number of orders whose swap at position $j$ is enabled. On seven labels put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cost}
 C(H)=\frac{a_1+2a_2+2a_3+2a_4+2a_5+a_6}{10}.
\end{equation}
The weights sum to one. They are chosen so that each interior swap is
counted with total weight one when a seven-position window slides
through an order.

\begin{lemma}[Exact finite estimate]\label{lem:finite}
There is a rational function $q$ on the isomorphism types of $\F_6$ such
that, for every $J\in\F_7$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite}
 C(J)\le\frac58|J|+
 \sum_{H\text{ bottom slice}}q(H)-\sum_{H\text{ top slice}}q(H).
\end{equation}
The rational table and exact verifiers are supplied in the certificate
supplement. The finite statement includes all fixed-pair constraints,
with no common-axis assumption.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We separate the certificate, its exceptional receiver case and its
coverage. The supplement gives the complete tables and replay instructions.

\emph{Certificate.}
The certificate assigns per-order weights $\phi$ to five-label types.
For a six-domain $H$ let $b(H)$ and $t(H)$ be the sums of
$|L|\phi(L)$ over its bottom and top five-slices. Put
\[
 s(H)=\frac{12}{19}|H|+b(H)-t(H)-\frac15 A(H),\qquad \eta=\frac1{152}.
\]
There are specified exceptional sets $\mathcal T,\mathcal S$ of five and
nine six-label types. Put $p(H)=2\eta(r_t(H)-r_b(H))$ outside $\mathcal S$,
and $p(H)=\psi(H)$ on $\mathcal S$, with the rational values $\psi$ supplied
by the certificate. Set $\theta_b=s/2+p$ and $\theta_t=s/2-p$.
The finite facts are as follows. On $\mathcal T$
both split values are zero. Outside $\mathcal T\cup\mathcal S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:receivers}
 \theta_b(H)\ge\frac\eta2|H|+4\eta r_t(H),\qquad
 \theta_t(H)\ge\frac\eta2|H|+4\eta r_b(H).
\end{equation}
Here $r_b,r_t$ count specified five-slices described below. A further
finite check proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lambda}
 \Lambda(J):=\sum_{\text{bottom }H}\theta_b(H)
             +\sum_{\text{top }H}\theta_t(H)\ge\eta|J|
\end{equation}
whenever $J$ has a slice in $(\mathcal T\setminus\{R_8\})\cup\mathcal S$.
All types, weights and slice selectors are explicitly defined in the
ancillary data; they are not fitted during verification.

\emph{The remaining exceptional type.} The only type not handled by
the explicit seven-label check is
\[
 R_8=\{524130,524310,534120,534210,
        542130,542310,543120,543210\}.
\]
It has a common first and last alternative. The quantity $r_b(H)$ counts
bottom five-slices of the type $R_8$ with its first alternative deleted;
$r_t(H)$ counts top five-slices of the type with its last deleted.
Both counts vanish on $R_8$.
If a bottom $R_8$ slice occurs, its eight orders share their first
alternative, so they enter one top receiver slice and contribute one
to its $r_b$. That receiver is not $R_8$. Distinct bottom slices give
distinct receiver/label pairs. Each contributes $4\eta$, exactly paying
the missing baseline $8\eta/2$. Top $R_8$ slices are symmetric.
In the remaining cases all non-$R_8$ slices lie outside
$\mathcal T\cup\mathcal S$, so~\eqref{eq:receivers} applies to every receiver.
It proves~\eqref{eq:lambda} in every case not handled by the exceptional finite check.

Define
\[
 q(H)=\frac{b(H)+t(H)}2+p(H).
\]
In a seven-domain, a top slice of a bottom slice is the same conditional
five-domain as the corresponding bottom slice of a top slice.
These middle terms cancel. Also the sum of $A(H)$ over all six-slices
is $10C(J)$. Consequently
\[
 \Lambda(J)=\frac{12}{19}|J|-C(J)
       +\sum_{\text{bottom }H}q(H)-\sum_{\text{top }H}q(H).
\]
Now~\eqref{eq:lambda} and $12/19-1/152=5/8$ give~\eqref{eq:finite}.

\emph{Coverage.} We finally explain why the finite checks are exhaustive.
All $128\,400$ labelled five-domains form $1\,160$ relabelling types.
Extend their defining laws over the ten new triples and five new pairs.
This gives $14\,105\,084$ nonempty six-extensions of five-type
representatives and $94\,830$ feature rows. The extension count is not a
count of distinct labelled six-domains.
Every six-domain occurs up to relabelling: restrict its \emph{defining
conditions} to five labels, rather than taking its projection.
Keeping every domain on the thirteen exceptional feature rows gives precisely the
fourteen required types. Two of the five types in $\mathcal T$ share one feature row.
Their representatives and reversals, omitting
$R_8$, give eighteen seven-label seed families. Requiring a prescribed
endpoint six-slice and intersecting all compatible law and pair options
produces $14\,248$ seed--domain cases (before deduplication across seeds)
and $18\,316$ feature rows, covering both
orientations. Every required inequality is verified by clearing the rational
denominators and checking a nonnegative integer residual. A separate
tuple-set checker recomputes every saved row from its witness orders.
The coverage and receiver arguments above are mathematical parts of the
proof; exact arithmetic verifies the finite residuals.
\end{proof}

\section{Cancellation, concatenation and the bounds}\label{sec:finish}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:density}
Every $K\in\F_n$ satisfies $\del(K)\ge3n|K|/8$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $n\ge7$, condition on the entire order outside each block of seven
consecutive positions. The conditional domain belongs to $\F_7$:
internal triples retain their extreme laws and crossing triples can
only impose pair comparisons or constant restrictions.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finite} to all these windows, with their outside
multiplicities. After regrouping by the exterior, the bottom six-slices of a window
starting at position $i$ are the top six-slices of the window starting
at $i-1$. Thus every interior potential cancels.
Since $\F_6$ is finite, $|q(H)|\le Q|H|$ for a constant $Q$;
the two endpoint layers contribute at most $2Q|K|$.

The weights in~\eqref{eq:cost} count each interior enabled swap once.
At the ends they miss at most five incidences per order: the total
coefficient deficit is $(n-1)-(n-6)=5$. Therefore
\[
 A(K)\le\frac58(n-6)|K|+(5+2Q)|K|
       \le\frac58n|K|+B|K|
\]
for a fixed $B$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:fibre},
$\del(K)\ge(3n/8-B)|K|$.

The boundary loss disappears by taking ordered copies. Fix any
$K\in\F_n$, with $M$ orders, and concatenate $t$ copies on disjoint
blocks, every alternative of an earlier block above every alternative
of a later block. Fixed cross-block pairs and compatible extreme
laws make this a member $K^{[t]}\in\F_{tn}$. Directly,
\[
 |K^{[t]}|=M^t,\qquad
 \del(K^{[t]})=t\del(K)M^{t-1}.
\]
Apply the affine bound to $K^{[t]}$ and divide by $tM^{t-1}$:
$\del(K)\ge(3n/8-B/t)M$. Let $t$ tend to infinity.
This also covers the original small values of $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Proposition~\ref{prop:density} supplies~\eqref{eq:alpha} with
$\alpha=3/8$. Proposition~\ref{prop:transfer} gives the two recurrences.
Iterate from $f(3)=h(3)=4$ to obtain the exponential bounds.

Ordered concatenation gives supermultiplicativity of both maxima.
Fekete's lemma applied to their logarithms proves existence of the
stated limits. Finally, retaining the factor $n/(n+8/11)$ gives, for $n\ge3$,
\[
 f(n)\le4(32/11)^{n-3}\prod_{k=4}^{n}\frac{k}{k+8/11}.
\]
The logarithm of the product is $-(8/11)\log n+O(1)$, which proves
the polynomial correction (the product is empty when $n=3$).
\end{proof}
